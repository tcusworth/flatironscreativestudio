Many agencies grow by adding layers: account managers, project managers, a bench of junior staff doing the production work. That structure suits the agency's growth. It doesn't always suit the client's project.

We run Flatirons Creative Studio differently on purpose. The model is simple: senior practitioners do the work, and you deal with them directly.

## What a layer costs you

Every layer between you and the person doing the work has a cost. Context gets lost in each hand-off. Decisions wait for someone to relay them. Feedback travels through a summary instead of being heard first-hand. And you pay for the coordination as well as the work.

## What "senior" changes

Experience shows up in judgment more than in speed. Someone who has built many sites knows which requests will cause trouble later, which corners are safe to cut, and when the honest answer is "you don't need that." That judgment is hard to supply through process documents.

It also means fewer meetings about the work and more of the work itself.

## What you give up

Staying small has trade-offs, and it is better to state them. A small studio takes on fewer projects at a time, so we are not the right fit for every timeline or every scale. If you need a large team working in parallel across many workstreams, a larger firm may suit you better, and we would rather tell you that on the first call than discover it halfway through.

## What we think is worth the trade

For most of the projects we take on, such as websites, search and AI visibility work, and custom tools, the quality of the thinking matters more than the headcount. Keeping the studio small keeps the thinking close to the work.
