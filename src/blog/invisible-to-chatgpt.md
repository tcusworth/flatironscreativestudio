Search the web for your own business and you probably appear. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity the question your customers ask, and you may not appear at all. That gap is easy to miss, because nothing in your analytics tells you about it.

The reason is that AI assistants don't find and rank brands the way a search engine does. They draw on a different set of signals.

## Two ways an assistant "knows" about you

An AI assistant can get information about your business from two broad places. The first is what the underlying model absorbed during training. The second is information retrieved at the moment of the question, when the assistant searches the web or reads pages on your behalf.

Different products lean on these in different proportions, and they change over time. What stays constant is the practical consequence: your brand needs to be described clearly and consistently in many places, and your own site needs to be easy for a machine to read.

## What tends to make a brand hard for AI to describe

- **An unclear description of what you do.** If your homepage leads with a slogan and never says plainly what you sell, to whom, and where, an assistant has little to repeat.
- **Inconsistent descriptions across the web.** If your website, directory listings and profiles each describe you differently, the picture an assistant assembles is blurry.
- **Few independent mentions.** Assistants tend to trust what other credible sources say about you more than what you say about yourself.
- **Pages a machine can't easily read.** Content locked inside images or scripts, or missing structured data, is harder to interpret.
- **Crawlers blocked by accident.** Some sites block AI crawlers in their robots.txt file, sometimes deliberately and sometimes by a default setting nobody reviewed. Each AI company publishes the names of its crawlers, so check their current documentation rather than a copied list.

## What to do about it

1. **Ask the assistants directly.** Put the questions your customers would ask to several assistants and write down what each says about you, your competitors and your category. This is your baseline.
2. **Write one plain description of your business** and use it everywhere: your homepage, your About page and your profiles. Name the services, the audience and the location.
3. **Add structured data** (schema markup) to your site so the basics, such as your organization, services and location, are stated explicitly rather than implied.
4. **Check your robots.txt** to confirm you are not blocking crawlers you want to be found by.
5. **Earn independent mentions**: client write-ups, credible directories, and press or partner pages that describe you the way you describe yourself.
6. **Re-check on a schedule.** AI answers change, so a one-time check goes stale.

None of this replaces good search engine optimization. Clear content, solid technical foundations and credible links help in both worlds. The difference is the extra step of checking what the assistants actually say, instead of assuming a good Google ranking carries over.
